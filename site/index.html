Reset Protocol

Pass it.Solve it.Don't shake it.

A ball-shaped bomb ticks down in your hands. Solve its puzzle with the keys, knobs, buttons and your voice, then get rid of it before the clock runs out. Shake it too hard and it goes off.

Everything happens on the ball

No laptop, no base station, no screen to look at. One ESP32 inside the ball runs the whole game: the puzzles, the clock, the lights, the sound and the buzz in your hands. Switch it on and play.

Game design · Saboteur mode

Same puzzle. Who's faking it?

A hidden-role mode for 4 to 8 players. Everyone does the same short puzzle on the bomb, one after another, while the rest of the table watches. One of them is secretly working for the bomb, and has to slip an extra move into their turn without anyone noticing.

  1. 1The puzzle

    The bomb picks one puzzle for the round and shows everyone how to do it.

  2. 2The secret check

    The ball goes round. Each player looks alone. The saboteur also gets a secret extra move, like pressing one key twice.

  3. 3Everyone goes

    15 seconds each. The saboteur's extra move only counts on their own turn. When it lands, the ball buzzes in their hands.

  4. 4Who was it?

    The bomb never says who slipped. Half of all turns get a fake buzz too, so a buzz alone proves nothing.

  5. 5The secret vote

    Everyone votes in private on the ball. Most votes is out. A tie means one revote, then the bomb rolls a die.

Civilians

Do the puzzle right and watch every hand on the ball.

  • A slip costs nothing: press X and try again
  • Watch for the extra move, not the mistakes
  • Vote out the saboteur to win
The saboteur

Do the puzzle right too, and sneak in the extra move.

  • Skip it and you're caught: out, no vote
  • Each civilian voted out burns the fuse
  • Win when it's down to you and one other

11 puzzles, 3 secret moves each

Every puzzle has its own set of sabotages, so the thing to watch changes every round. Each puzzle comes up once before any repeats.

01Shout on the blocks 02Turn both knobs 03Tap the keys 04Copy the lights 05Keep it level 06Do the sum 07Type the mix 08Remember the lights 09Turn to the mark 10Stop in the box 11Shake it 3 times

More ways to play

Four modes on one ball

Pick a mode, a player count and a difficulty on the bomb itself. The colour you wear on your headband is the colour the bomb calls.

2 to 8 playersClassic

Solve your puzzle before the clock runs out, or gamble on a pass. Explode and you're out. Last one standing wins.

4 to 8 playersBomber

Secret bombers mark crew members for a panic round. Defuse enough puzzles before they take over.

4 to 8 playersSaboteur

Same puzzle for everyone. One of you adds a secret move. Find them.

4 to 8 playersSecret rule

Everyone knows where the token should land, except one. Move it, blend in, vote.

How we built the bomb, from cardboard to the ball.

31 Oct

Off to alt.ctrl.GDC

We sent our application to alt.ctrl.GDC 2027, with a full set of drawings for the demo space: a pedestal for the bomb, a striped blast ring on the floor and eight coloured spots to stand on.

Event
30 Oct

The saboteur gets a buzz

Our hidden-role mode went through four versions in two days. The one that stuck: everyone does the same puzzle, and the saboteur adds one extra move. A buzz tells them it worked. Fake buzzes on half the turns keep everyone guessing.

Design
30 Oct

Secret rule mode

A 4 by 4 grid on the screen, a token and a rule only some of you know. Leave it on a red square, or next to a dotted one. The player who doesn't know has to copy the others and hope.

Design
28 Oct

Bomber mode

Our first hidden-role mode. Bombers mark crew members during their turns, then the alarm goes off and someone gets a panic round.

Design
27 Oct

The look

Matte black all over, one yellow band round the middle, and the logo badge on top. The shell is a collapsible disc ball, so the panels can flex when it's passed.

Art
25 Oct

It blew itself up

With the ball closed, the motor's own 3-2-1 buzz shook the motion sensor hard enough to set the bomb off. We raised the threshold instead of hiding the motor, so the bomb can still blow up during the countdown.

Playtest
25 Oct

Difficulty and action puzzles

Easy, Medium and Hard. Plus six action puzzles drawn on the tiny screen: Pong with a knob for each paddle, a car you steer by tilting the ball, balloons you pop by shouting.

Code
24 Oct

First playtests

Elimination rules, a coin-flip pass gamble that gets riskier every time, and a hand-over moment where the ball calls the next colour. Players started guarding the ball like it was real.

Playtest
23 Oct

First casualty

The USB chip on our main board died. A short somewhere on the perfboard. The spare board stepped in while we hunted for it.

Hardware
21 Oct

One board to rule them all

Everything moved from the breadboard mess onto one soldered core board that fits inside the ball, with a plug for every part.

Hardware
20 Oct

It speaks

8-bit sound made by the ESP32 itself, no sound files. It took an evening to work out why the board went dead: a software update, not our code.

Code
14 to 16 Oct

A ball that ticks

The first idea: a bomb you pass around a party, solving quick puzzles on it. Notebook sketches, a parts list from the lab and one blinking blue LED.
