Ticking Bomb Bench Wiring

How the 30-pin ESP32, the 16x2 LCD, one button and one LED connect. The ESP32 sits off the breadboard, because it's too wide to fit. Pin names match the labels printed on your board.

What the symbols mean

ResistorLimits current. The 330 Ω one stops the LED from burning out. It works either way round.
LEDLights up. Current only flows the way the triangle points: long leg (+) on the triangle side, short leg (−) on the bar side.
Push buttonThe bar is the button's top. Pressing it joins the two contacts, so RX2 is connected to ground.
Dot on a lineA real connection. Lines that cross without a dot just pass over each other.
RailsThe long + and − strips down the side of your breadboard. Anything plugged into a strip shares it.
Board pinsYellow means used in this test. Empty circles are free for later.

F = female end (a socket, slides onto a pin). M = male end (a pin, plugs into the breadboard). Total: 6 F–M, 2 F–F, 2 M–M. Have a few spares.

Breadboard rules

  • Never connect a button or an ESP32 pin to the + rail. It carries 5V and the ESP32's pins are 3.3V.
  • Unplug USB before moving wires.
  • The ESP32 sits off the breadboard. Rest it on its foam or cardboard so its bare pins never touch each other or anything metal.
  • Small 4-leg buttons: use two diagonally opposite legs.
  • LED: long leg toward D33, short leg to ground. Backwards, it stays dark.

If something's off

  • LCD backlight on but no text: turn the blue screw on the back slowly.
  • Serial Monitor says no I2C device: check SDA and SCL aren't swapped.
  • Garbage in the Serial Monitor: set it to 115200 baud.
  • The LCD's I2C board pulls SDA and SCL up to 5V. Fine for bench tests. Add a level shifter for the final build.
